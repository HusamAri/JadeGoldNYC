Notes on the rows: Zoeys and JewelrymineUSA publish one weight per width without a length. Zoeys' Franco weights
(28.3 g, 41.5 g, 65 g) are the same numbers as our supplier catalog's 18 in weights, so they are read as 18 in;

Read: US shops that publish weights sell heavy chains at 6.1 to 8.0 USD/g and light pieces at 9 to 13 USD/g.
The floor of the market is 4.6 to 5 USD/g (JewelrymineUSA on a few sizes, shops shipping from Turkey or India).
One outlier at 2.7 USD/g (SilverJewelrycoUSA, 5 reviews, 6 mm 24 in 64.4 g for 175.00) is below today's melt
plus making and is treated as old stock. The volume leaders without published weights (ClaudJewelers 20.7k,
LarkKing 24.4k) sell lighter gauges: Claud's 4 mm Franco is 210.00 to 253.40 over 16 to 24 in, its 6.5 mm Figaro
101.50 to 157.50, both under our price for the same width because our chains carry more silver.

Read: at 5.00 USD/g cost Leander now sells at 6.4 to 7.7 USD/g for anything above 8 g, which is the low end of
the US market per gram and level with the 22.7k-review leader on heavy Franco. Per piece it is cheaper than the
leaders on bracelets and thin chains and dearer than the light-gauge sellers on wide chains, because the supplier's
chains are heavier for their width. It is not the cheapest shop on Etsy and cannot be at this cost: break-even
after Etsy fees alone is 5.52 USD/g plus 9.90 per order.

## The lever is the supplier price, not the tag

5.00 USD/g is 3.4 times melt (1.49). The leaders sell heavy chain at 6.1 to 6.8 USD/g behind 50%-off banners and
keep a margin, which is only possible with a wholesale price well under ours. Every 0.50 USD/g off the supplier
price lowers every list price by 0.74 USD/g (8 to 10% on the heavy variants). At 3.50 USD/g the same rule puts the
4.1 mm 18 in Franco at 192 instead of 269, 31% under Zoeys.
